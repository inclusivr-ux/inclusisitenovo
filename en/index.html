Connecting worlds through sign languages and technology.

InclusiVR is an EdTech startup born at the University of São Paulo (USP). We teach LIBRAS (Brazilian Sign Language) through playful books, apps and games set in Signária, a city where everyone signs.

The problem.

Learning LIBRAS is still hard, and those who need it most start late.

90%of deaf children are born to hearing families.

  • These families need to learn LIBRAS to talk with their child from the start.
  • People who study alone see the sign on video, but can't tell if they are signing correctly.
  • Brazilian law recognizes LIBRAS (Law 10,436/2002), includes it in teacher and speech therapist training (Decree 5,626/2005) and guarantees bilingual education for deaf students (Law 14,191/2021).

Source: Mitchell, R. E.; Karchmer, M. A. Chasing the mythical ten percent: parental hearing status of deaf and hard of hearing students in the United States. Sign Language Studies, 4(2), 138–163, 2004. US data: fewer than 5% of deaf students have a deaf parent.

Our approach.

Pedagogy, storytelling and technology, built with the deaf community: more than 250 interviews with deaf and hearing people (2023–2025).

  • Show, try, repeat

    The sign is shown, the learner tries, gets instant feedback and repeats (Applied Behavior Analysis).

  • Sign, word and image

    The sign, the written word and the object in the scene appear together (stimulus equivalence).

  • Many senses

    See, touch and do: texture, images, 3D and movement (multisensory learning).

  • A good story

    The narrative gives a reason to practice: helping Aia and Seu Zé find the missing animals.

Technology as support. It complements teachers and interpreters; it never replaces them. For us, LIBRAS is a language, a culture and a creative force, not just an accessibility feature.
